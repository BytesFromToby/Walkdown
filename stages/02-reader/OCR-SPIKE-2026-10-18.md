# OCR spike (2026-10-18)

**Question:** can OCR become pip-only, so Tesseract stops being an addon a user has to install?

**Answer:** no, not on this evidence. RapidOCR, the strongest pip-only candidate, reads less than
Tesseract on the cases that matter (near-invisible text), at about 9 times the time and about
350 MB more on disk. Two things did come out of it, and both keep Tesseract as the only optional
OCR tool: **pypdfium2** (pip, 3.6 MB) renders PDF pages for OCR with no poppler, and
**better preprocessing** lifts Tesseract more than changing engines would.

Evidence, not doctrine: the harness lives outside the repo; numbers below are what it printed.

## Candidates

| | Tesseract 5.3.4 (current) | RapidOCR 3.10 (ONNX, PP-OCRv6 small) | pypdfium2 5.14 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Install | system package (apt, brew, Windows installer) + `pytesseract` | pip: rapidocr, onnxruntime, opencv-python-headless, numpy, shapely, pyclipper, omegaconf | pip |
| Size | binary + English data, outside Python | about 125 MB of wheels, about 350 MB installed (OpenCV 150, numpy 70, onnxruntime 68, models 33) | 3.6 MB wheel |
| License | Apache-2.0 | Apache-2.0 (models Apache-2.0; onnxruntime MIT; OpenCV Apache-2.0) | BSD-3-Clause / Apache-2.0 |
| Deterministic (3 runs per image, CPU) | yes, 22 of 22 identical | yes, 22 of 22 identical | n/a |
| Time per image | 0.2 s | 1.5 to 1.7 s | n/a |

## Test set

22 cases. The stage 2 fixture image (`class11-image-text.png`, truth read off the image, not the
answer sheet), and 21 synthetic ones drawn with DejaVu fonts, each a hostile-style English line:
text at 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 24 px; gray `#999`, `#CCC`, and `#EEE` on white (the last is near
invisible to a person); white on `#F5F5F5`; blue on navy; dark mode; serif; a monospace code
screenshot; a blurred copy; a 90-degree rotation; a gradient background; a three-line paragraph;
9 px text in the corner of a 2400 x 1600 image; 8 px light-gray text in a 2400 x 1600 image; and
an image-only PDF (a scanned page), rendered with pypdfium2 at 2x.

Score: share of the truth's words found in the OCR output. "Read" means 90% or more.

## Results

| Case | Tesseract (current prep) | RapidOCR (raw) | Tesseract + prep2 | RapidOCR + prep2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#EEE` on white | 1.00 | **0.00** | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| white on `#F5F5F5` | 1.00 | **0.00** | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 9 px in a large image | 0.64 | 0.00 | **1.00** | 1.00 |
| 8 px light gray in a large image | 0.23 | 0.00 | **0.92** | 0.85 |
| 6 px | 0.27 | 0.64 | 0.27 | 0.55 |
| 8 px | 0.91 | 0.91 | 0.91 | 0.64 |
| rotated 90 degrees | 0.00 | 0.45 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| fixture image | 0.80 | 0.87 | 0.80 | 0.87 |
| image-only PDF (pypdfium2) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.89 |
| the other 13 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| **Read, of 22** | **17** | **15** | **19** | **16** |

`prep2`: grayscale; crop to what differs from the most common (background) value; stretch the
contrast (`autocontrast`); invert dark backgrounds; enlarge the crop 3x when it is under 2000 px.
Today's `for_ocr` only grays and enlarges small images, so a small snippet in a large image is
read at its natural size.

Crop has one hole: two small snippets in opposite corners of a large image give a crop the size
of the whole image (0.70). Tiling the image into 600 px tiles with overlap, and reading only the
tiles that are not blank, reads both (1.00, 0.3 s).

## What this means

1. **Keep Tesseract as the one optional OCR tool.** Pip-only OCR costs more and reads less where
   hiding happens. A model reads near-invisible text without trouble, so an OCR that drops it is
   the wrong stand-in.
2. **Build PDF render + OCR on pypdfium2** (core, in `requirements.txt`): render each page, OCR it
   when Tesseract is present, compare with the text layer. Closes the "not built: PDF render"
   item with no poppler. Without Tesseract, it is a skip, as images are today.
3. **Replace `for_ocr` with prep2 plus tiling.** The largest gain in the spike, and a hiding route
   it closes (small text in a large image) is cheap to use.
4. **Still open:** rotated text (neither engine reads it; trying four orientations with Tesseract
   costs 4x), and text under 8 px.

The finding of the image check stays the pointer (stage 5, "an image the model is pointed at"):
OCR text is a deterministic lower bound on what a model reads, and models differ.
